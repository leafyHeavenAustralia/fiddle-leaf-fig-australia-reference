A quick-lookup tool for Ficus lyrata: diagnose common leaf symptoms, adjust watering checks by Australian setting, choose safer light positions, and compare standard plants with the compact ‘Bambino’ form.

1. Symptom diagnosis lookup

Start with the pattern of damage, then check moisture, light, temperature and recent changes before changing multiple variables at once.

What you seeLikely cause / contextFirst check or actionPrevention
Sudden all-over leaf dropRelocation, cold draught or rapid environmental changeStabilise the position; correct temperature/light issues.Choose one suitable position and avoid repeated moves.
Lower leaves yellow then dropExcess moisture or root stressLet the mix dry appropriately; inspect drainage and roots.Water from actual mix dryness, not the calendar.
Crispy brown edgesDry root zone, dry indoor air or excessive direct sunWater thoroughly when due; move away from harsh afternoon exposure.Keep moisture more consistent and use filtered light.
Stippled/bronzed leaves + fine webbingSpider mitesIsolate; wash foliage; use a labelled treatment according to its directions.Inspect leaves regularly and keep conditions suitable.
Small brown/tan bumps + sticky residueScale insectsIsolate and physically remove light infestations; use a labelled treatment for heavier infestations.Check stems, veins and leaf undersides on new plants.
Brown/black leaf spots, sometimes with yellowingLeaf-spot disease or other stressRemove badly affected tissue; improve airflow; avoid routinely wetting leaves.Keep foliage dry where possible and avoid stagnant, overly humid conditions.
One older lower leaf drops occasionallyNormal ageing can occur; not automatically a crisis.Look at new growth and whether the problem is spreading.Judge the overall trend rather than reacting to one old leaf.
Leaf drop after bringing the plant homeChange in light, temperature, airflow or humidityGive it a stable position; avoid heavy corrective watering or fertilising.Choose the permanent position before making several changes.

These are checking intervals, not mandatory watering schedules. The potting mix should decide when you actually water.

Subtropical — Brisbane and northern NSWCheck every few days to about weekly, depending on light and pot size.Often less frequently than summer.Air conditioning can dry foliage/air while cool weather slows the mix.

5. Pruning, repotting and propagation

Repot

Spring / active growth

Increase pot size gradually. Oversized containers can stay wet for too long.

Prune safely

Protect skin

Wear gloves when pruning or repotting because Ficus sap can irritate skin.

Propagate

Use a node

Stem cuttings and air layering are practical methods. A leaf without stem tissue and a viable node is not a dependable home-propagation method.

Tall / bare plant

Consider air layering

Air layering can form roots on the stem before a rooted section is separated, making it useful when shortening a tall plant.

7. Fast rules worth remembering

  • Diagnose before watering, fertilising or repotting.
  • Use the plant’s actual mix dryness—not the calendar—as the watering trigger.
  • Bright filtered light is a safer indoor baseline than harsh afternoon exposure.
  • One stable position is usually better than repeated moves after every symptom.
  • When several problems appear at once, correct one environmental variable at a time.
  • Keep plants away from chewing pets and children; treat ingestion as a poisoning/exposure issue.
